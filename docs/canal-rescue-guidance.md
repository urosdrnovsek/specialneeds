# Duck’s Canal Rescue: clearer actions and scene

The game now shows one current move above the canal, with a picture pair, a short action sentence, and matching source/destination outlines in the scene. A static dashed route illustrates moves without playing them for the child. A small rescue plan shows Load, Lift, Open gate, Sail and Unload; the current phase is marked with text and contrast.

The normal sequence is: move Duck and the parcel into the boat in either order; pump twice to lift the water; open the gate; move the boat to the landing; unload Duck and the parcel separately. Pumping and opening the gate cannot complete the rescue. If the water is raised too early, the instruction explicitly says to close the gate and lower the water before loading. Extra water/gate experiments remain available in a collapsed panel.

The canal, brick lock, two wooden landings, little house, boat and bird-shaped duck were redrawn as SVG graphics. The same authored layout places the duck’s feet and parcel base on their supporting surfaces, and is reused for course artwork. Main object labels are readable and the highlighted next action changes as the state changes. Optional controls do not occupy the main scene.

This supports adult-led cause-and-effect play, following a visible sequence and choosing an accessible input method. Children can drag, or select an object and then its destination; no speech or sound is required. It is not an assessment or evaluated intervention. Existing progress and unrelated games are retained.

Validation: purposeful-play regressions in automatic and manual pacing, direct-route startup, new instruction/recovery/order checks and nine static support/contact states; reading and parent navigation regressions; complete artwork inventory/contact checks. Nine canal scene states were rasterised and visually inspected. Static SVG review and DOM drag descriptors do not establish browser layout, CSS hit testing or physical touch behaviour; those remain unverified in the current tool environment.
